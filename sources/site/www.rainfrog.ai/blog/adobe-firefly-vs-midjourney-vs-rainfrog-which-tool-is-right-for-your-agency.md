## The Short Answer: Which Tool for Which Agency

Choose **Adobe Firefly** if your team lives in Photoshop and Premiere and needs commercially safe generation inside an existing licence. Choose **Midjourney** if your primary output is concept art, moodboards, and single hero images where aesthetic ceiling matters most. Choose **Rainfrog** if you deliver multi-asset campaigns and the consistency between images is the deliverable.

Most agencies end up using two of the three. The mistake is expecting one tool to cover all three jobs.

### The three jobs, named clearly

**Job 1 — Ideation.** Moodboards, pitch visuals, territory exploration. Nobody ships these. Speed and range beat precision. Midjourney and Firefly Boards both do this well.

**Job 2 — Retouching and extension.** Removing objects, expanding crops, generative fill on existing photography. This is Adobe's home turf and has been since Photoshop shipped Generative Fill.

**Job 3 — Campaign production.** Twenty assets for the same product across six formats, all recognisably the same shoot. This is where general-purpose generators consistently underperform, which is the case we've made in [what "campaign-level" AI image generation actually means](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/what-campaign-level-ai-image-generation-actually-means).

## What Each Tool Is Actually Built For

Firefly is a generative layer inside a creative suite. Midjourney is a research lab that sells access to a model. Rainfrog is a campaign production system that happens to use image models. Those origin stories predict almost every practical difference you'll hit in a real project.

**Adobe Firefly** was launched as the commercially safe generative layer for Creative Cloud — trained on licensed and public-domain content so agencies could use output in client work without indemnity anxiety. Adobe has since [opened Firefly to partner models](https://www.adobe.com/products/firefly/partner-models.html) including GPT Image 2, Google's Gemini 3 with Nano Banana, Kling AI, and Runway, turning it into an aggregator as much as a model.

**Midjourney** remains the aesthetic benchmark. It is also the least integrated of the three: no client-facing collaboration layer, no asset management, no brand memory. It renders, beautifully, and hands you a file.

**Rainfrog** starts from the opposite end. You define products, characters, styles, and environments once, then mix and match them to generate a campaign — no prompt engineering. The unit of work is a set, not a shot. That design choice is the whole argument, laid out in [how Rainfrog generates campaign visuals without a single prompt](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/how-rainfrog-generates-campaign-visuals-without-a-single-prompt).

## Adobe Firefly: Pricing, Strengths, and Limits in 2026

Firefly runs on a generative-credit model across four individual tiers: Standard at US$9.99/mo (2,000 credits), Pro at US$19.99/mo (4,000 credits), Pro Plus at US$49.99/mo (10,000 credits), and Premium at US$199.99/mo (50,000 credits), per [Adobe's official plan comparison](https://www.adobe.com/products/firefly/plans.html). Team licences are available at the Pro, Pro Plus, and Premium price points.

Every paid tier includes unlimited access to standard image features like Generative Fill; credits are consumed by premium features such as text-to-video and partner-model generations.

### Where Firefly wins for agencies

**Commercial safety and provenance.** Firefly's training-data position and Content Credentials support remain the strongest legal story of the three. For regulated clients — finance, pharma, public sector — this alone decides the tool.

**It's already in the workflow.** Generative Fill inside Photoshop is a retouching feature, not a separate app. No new tab, no new file-handoff step, no new seat to justify.

**Admin tooling.** Team plans include admin controls and 24×7 support, which matters once more than five people are generating client-facing assets.

### Where Firefly falls short

**Credits become the planning unit.** Once premium models and video enter the workflow, teams start rationing generations rather than iterating freely — a friction we've documented in our [Adobe Firefly review of pricing and limits](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/adobe-firefly-review-pricing-limits-alternatives).

**No native campaign consistency.** Firefly gives you Generative Match and style references, but keeping one product and one model consistent across twenty assets is still manual work you assemble shot by shot. For the direct comparison, see [Rainfrog vs Adobe Firefly](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/rainfrog-vs-adobe-firefly-a-head-to-head-for-creative-agencies-2026).

## Midjourney: Pricing, Strengths, and Limits in 2026

Midjourney sells GPU time across four tiers: Basic at $10/mo (3.3 fast GPU hours), Standard at $30/mo (15 hours plus unlimited Relax-mode images), Pro at $60/mo (30 hours), and Mega at $120/mo (60 hours), according to [Midjourney's official plan comparison](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/27870484040333-Comparing-Midjourney-Plans). Extra fast time costs $4/hour, and annual billing saves 20%.

Two details matter for agencies specifically. Stealth Mode — which keeps generations private rather than public — is only available on Pro and Mega. And Midjourney's terms require companies earning more than $1,000,000 USD in gross annual revenue to subscribe at Pro or Mega.

### Where Midjourney wins

**Aesthetic ceiling.** For a single striking image — a pitch key visual, an editorial territory, a concept frame — Midjourney still produces work that reads as art-directed rather than generated.

**Cost per exploration.** Unlimited Relax-mode images from $30/mo makes broad visual exploration genuinely cheap. For moodboarding, that's excellent value.

### Where Midjourney falls short

**Client confidentiality is a paid upgrade.** If you're working on an unannounced product, Basic and Standard tiers publish your generations to the public gallery. That's a $60/mo floor for any agency under NDA.

**Prompt drift across a set.** Reproduce the same product across a series and small details wander — stitching, proportions, label placement. Character and style references help; they don't solve it. This is the core argument in [Rainfrog vs Midjourney](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/rainfrog-vs-midjourney-which-actually-works-for-campaign-production).

**No production layer.** No approvals, no versioning, no brand asset library. Everything downstream of generation is your problem.

## Rainfrog: Pricing, Strengths, and Limits in 2026

Rainfrog is built around reusable campaign components rather than prompts. You upload a product, define a character, pick a style and an environment, then generate a full set of visuals that share the same lighting, palette, and treatment. Current plans are listed on [rainfrog.ai/pricing](https://www.rainfrog.ai/pricing).

The platform came out of Pezzo di Studio, a working digital design agency — which is why the [Rainfrog workflow](https://www.rainfrog.ai/workflows) is organised around campaign delivery rather than around a prompt box.

### Where Rainfrog wins

**Consistency is the default, not the goal.** Because products, characters, styles, and environments are defined once and reused, the twentieth image belongs to the same campaign as the first. That's the difference between a folder of images and a deliverable.

**No prompt engineering tax.** Junior designers and account handlers produce usable output on day one. Agencies that have moved to component-based generation report campaign turnarounds dropping from weeks to days — the pattern behind [running a full visual campaign with AI from brief to final assets](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/how-to-run-a-full-visual-campaign-with-ai-2026).

**Built for briefs, not for prompts.** The input is closer to a creative brief than a text string, which we unpack in [how to brief an AI image generator like a creative director](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/how-to-brief-ai-image-generator-like-creative-director).

### Where Rainfrog falls short

**It's not a retoucher.** If your job today is removing a lamp post from a client photograph, Photoshop and Firefly are the right tools. Rainfrog generates campaigns; it isn't a pixel-editing suite.

**It's not a video platform.** For motion work, Runway and Firefly's video models are the relevant comparison — covered in [Rainfrog vs Runway](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/rainfrog-vs-runway-image-generation-vs-video).

**Set-up before speed.** Defining products and styles takes an hour up front. The payoff arrives on asset number six, not asset number one.

## How to Choose: A Decision Framework for Agencies

Don't start from the tool. Start from the deliverable you're most often late on. That single question resolves the choice faster than any feature matrix.

**1\. Count your assets per brief.** If a typical job ships one to three images, Midjourney or Firefly is enough. If it ships twelve or more across formats, consistency tooling stops being a nice-to-have.

**2\. Check who's generating.** If output depends on one person who's good at prompting, you have a bus-factor problem, not a tooling advantage. Component-based systems distribute the capability.

**3\. Audit your legal exposure.** Regulated clients push you toward Firefly's indemnity position regardless of aesthetic preference.

**4\. Price the confidentiality floor.** For NDA work, Midjourney's real entry price is $60/mo, not $10.

**5\. Time your revision loop.** The cost that kills margin isn't generation — it's the third round of revisions where the client asks for "the same but in the autumn palette." Systems that hold components constant make that a re-render. Prompt-based tools make it a restart.

## The Cost Nobody Puts in the Comparison Spreadsheet

Every one of these tools is cheap relative to a photoshoot. The expensive line item is the rework loop between "generated" and "approved," and it doesn't appear on any pricing page.

The [IAB found](https://www.iab.com/news/outlook-study-forecasts-9-5-growth-in-u-s-ad-spend/) that cost efficiency is now the top-cited benefit of AI creative in 2026 — up from fifth place in 2024. But efficiency measured at the generation step is misleading. [McKinsey's analysis of generative AI in marketing](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/how-generative-ai-can-boost-consumer-marketing) puts the productivity opportunity at 5–15% of total marketing spend — value that only lands when the workflow, not just the tool, changes.

The governance side is where it bites. Writing in [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/garydrenik/2026/07/23/ai-content-growth-is-outpacing-brand-governance/), Gary Drenik argues that AI content volume is now outpacing brands' ability to keep it on-brand — the volume problem has been solved and the control problem hasn't. Envato's [Beyond Adoption: The State of AI in Creative Work 2026](https://elements.envato.com/learn/ai-trend-report) reaches a similar conclusion: adoption that requires heavy editing doesn't hold.

That's the honest frame for this comparison. All three tools generate competent images in 2026. The question is how many of them survive review — a point we make at length in [why AI image generation fails for campaigns](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/why-ai-image-generation-fails-for-campaigns).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use Adobe Firefly, Midjourney, and Rainfrog together?** Yes, and most agencies should. A common stack is Midjourney for pitch-stage exploration, [Rainfrog](https://www.rainfrog.ai/) for campaign production, and Firefly or Photoshop for final retouching. They solve different problems, so the overlap in monthly cost is small relative to the time saved.

**Which is cheapest for a small agency?** On sticker price, Firefly Standard at $9.99/mo. On real cost, it depends on volume. Midjourney's confidentiality requirement pushes NDA work to $60/mo, and Firefly's credit ceilings bite once video enters the mix. Compare against current [Rainfrog pricing](https://www.rainfrog.ai/pricing) using your actual monthly asset count, not the entry tier.

**Is Midjourney safe to use for paid client work?** Commercially, yes — Midjourney's terms grant broad usage rights to subscribers, though companies over $1M in gross annual revenue must be on Pro or Mega. For confidentiality, Basic and Standard publish generations publicly, so NDA work needs Stealth Mode.

**Does Adobe Firefly keep brand consistency across a campaign?** Partially. Style references and Generative Match help match a look, but holding a specific product or model consistent across a full asset set is still manual assembly. See [Rainfrog vs Adobe Firefly](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/rainfrog-vs-adobe-firefly-a-head-to-head-for-creative-agencies-2026) for a side-by-side on that specific job.

**What should an agency test first?** Run one real brief through each tool and measure rounds-to-approval, not generation time. The tool that gets you to client sign-off in fewest revisions is the one that saves money, regardless of subscription cost.

**Do I still need a photographer?** For hero brand imagery and anything requiring a real, recognisable person or location — usually yes. For product-in-context, seasonal variations, and channel resizes, increasingly no. That shift is mapped in [why DTC brands are replacing traditional photoshoots with AI](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/why-dtc-brands-are-replacing-traditional-photoshoots-with-ai-2026).
